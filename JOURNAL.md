# 10/23/2026 10 PM - Gluing Magnets and Picking a Joint

_Time spent: 5h_

So I started gluing the magnets in with gorilla super glue gel (from the inside so it doesnt get on the faces) and when I put two J7 keybits together they tilted up a little. I thought I had to sand it but it turned out that glue had gotten onto the outside face of some magnets so they werent flat, after scraping it off with a razor the tilt went away. Btw acetone doesnt hurt the magnets but it does wreck the resin so dont get it on the case.

J7 was the one I liked the most so I made it the main keybit case.

![image](assets/journal/j7_keybits_joined.jpeg)

The magnets kept deflecting when I pushed them in against the keyswitch so I also designed a jig that drops in where the switch goes so they all stop at exactly 0.75mm.

For the keyword the plain pocket J7 tray was too tight for the 4x2 magnets (probably resin curing tolerance) but the collar one worked. I also realized that I need at least 2 magnets per side on the keyword like on the keybit, and since the collars stick out, a 4x1 magnet never reaches into the tray so it doesnt hit the side contacts or the inductors anymore. That made the J7.1 and J7.1b keyword trays and also a press fit coupon to see if I could skip the glue completely.

I also priced out a full sized keyboard (2 keywords and like 110 keybits) and its around $900 assembled, almost half of that being the pogo pins lol, so if I ever make a full one ill buy them seperately and solder them myself.

# 10/22/2026 9 PM - Magnet Joint Tests

_Time spent: 6h_

So I went back to the magnets and did some math, every joint has 4 pogo pins at around 120gf each so its about 520 to 720gf of spring force pushing the cases apart, and the tiny 2mm x 0.5mm magnets I had only give like 1gf through the case wall which is why it kept falling apart.

I tried adding pockets for 4mm x 2mm magnets but on the keyword they would hit the side contacts and the inductors, so I switched to the 4mm x 1mm ones that I already had. Then I tried a bunch of other ways of holding them together and made several cases so I could test all of them:

- J1 magnets only
- J2 bow tie key that drops in from the top
- J3 snap hooks
- J4 sliding dovetail
- J5 slip pegs
- J6 crush rib pegs
- J7 protruding magnets

For J7 I made the magnets stick out 0.75mm so theyre the length of the pogo pins - 0.5mm, which leaves a 1.5mm gap between cases. The keybit uses 4mm x 3mm magnets and the keyword had 3 versions (J7 with 4x2, J7b with 4x1 on collars and J7c with 4x2 on collars). I printed 2 of each one on the formlabs form 4 and yea

# 10/21/2026 10 PM - Pogo Pin Force

_Time spent: 3h_

So after the first magnet cases I noticed the magnets werent strong enough to hold the modules together against the pogo pins. I spent some time looking on digikey for single right angle pogo pins with a low enough spring force that a 4mm x 1mm N52 magnet could hold it. The closest thing was the Mill-Max 0967 horizontal SMT pins with the low force springs, but they need a different footprint so I would have to redo the boards, so I decided to make the magnets stronger instead.
