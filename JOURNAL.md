# 11/04/2026 - Routing the Keybit Flex

So today I finished routing the whole new keybit. Its a cross shaped flex with the CH592F in the middle and a flap on each side that folds up into the case, and each flap has 6 magnet pads going N S N S N S plus its own little switch circuit. I routed the north flap first, then copied it to the other 3 sides and relinked the copies to their own parts in the schematic (south, east and west) so every side has its own nets.

![image](assets/journal/keybit_v2_full_routing.png)

The tricky part is the magnet voltage. Since you can put a key next to another one in any rotation or stagger, you dont know which magnet net (A or B) is going to be + and which is GND. So each side has 2 P-FETs (RV2C014BCT2CL) that connect a net to VBUS and 2 N-FETs (SSM3K56ACT) that connect a net to GND, with the gates crossed. Whichever net is higher turns on the P-FET to VBUS on its side and the N-FET to GND on the other side, so it acts like a bridge rectifier but without losing 0.6V per key like diodes would. Then 2 more small N-FETs on an EN pin let the CH592 decide if a side is allowed to send power out, while a dead key can still get power in through the body diodes. The 1M resistors keep the magnets at 0V when nothing touches them, and two RB520 diodes into a 100k/300k divider let each side sense if its getting power, which is how the keyword can figure out who is next to who.

![image](assets/journal/keybit_v2_side_schematic.png)

All the transistors are DFN1006 (1x0.6mm) which is tiny but its the only way all of this fits on a ~5mm flap. Here's a closer look at the south flap and the middle with the CH592, the crystal and the antenna in the corner with a keepout around it

![image](assets/journal/keybit_v2_flap_routing.png)

I also had to move a bunch of traces away from the folds since anything running along a bend will crack, and I switched the board to JLC's flex rules (1/3 oz copper, 0.025mm polyimide) so DRC checks against what they can actually make.

Then I started laying out the new keybit flex. Each of the 4 flaps has 6 magnet pads going N S N S N S (MAG_NA / MAG_NB) and a row of tiny DFN1006 mosfets under them that let a neighbour connect either way round, plus the debug pads (GND, TIO, TCK, 3V3) and the hot swap sockets on the middle part. I also switched the board to JLC's flex rules (2 layer, 1/3 oz copper, 0.025mm polyimide) so DRC actually checks against what they can make.

![image](assets/journal/keybit_flap_layout.png)

Heres how each side works. Every side has two nets, A (the 3 N magnets) and B (the 3 S magnets), and since a neighbour can touch you either way round (rotated or staggered), either one could end up being + or GND. So each side has 2 P-FETs (P1/P2, RV2C014BCT2CL) that connect a net to VBUS and 2 N-FETs (N1/N2, SSM3K56ACT) that connect a net to GND, with their gates crossed over. If A is higher, N2 sees A on its gate and pulls B to GND, and P1's gate gets pulled down to B so A connects to VBUS. If B is higher its the mirror image, so it basically acts like a bridge rectifier but without losing 0.6V per key like diodes would.

![image](assets/journal/side_switch_schematic.png)

On top of that theres E1/E2, two more small N-FETs on an EN pin from the CH592. When EN is low the 100k pull ups keep P1/P2 off, so power can still come in through the body diodes (a dead key can still boot from any side) but nothing goes out. When EN is high the side fully turns on both ways. The 1M resistors keep A and B at 0V when nothing is touching. Then theres a sense part per side, two RB520CS30L diodes from A and B into a 100k/300k divider going to a GPIO, so the key can tell which of its sides have power and the keyword can figure out which keys are next to each other by turning one side on at a time. Everything is DFN1006 (1x0.6mm) so it fits on the flaps, and I simulated it and the worst key on a 110 key board only drops about 0.6V as long as theres 2+ battery boards or USB feeding it.
